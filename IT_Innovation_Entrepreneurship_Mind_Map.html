01 · Recognising Business Opportunities
Opportunity vs. Idea
  • Opportunity — favourable circumstances creating a need; must be attractive, durable, timely, value-anchored
  • Idea — a possible means of pursuing an opportunity (many ideas per opportunity)
3 approaches to identifying opportunities
  • Observing trends — economic, social, technological, political/regulatory
  • Solving a problem — often personally experienced
  • Finding gaps in the marketplace — niches too small for mainstream players
4 trend categories
  • Economic forces (recession → discount/pay-later models)
  • Social forces (aging population, mobile use, wellness)
  • Technological advances (new categories spawn industries)
  • Political/regulatory change (compliance-driven opportunity)
Personal characteristics that aid recognition
  • Prior industry experience
  • Cognitive factors — "entrepreneurial alertness"
  • Social networks — weak ties beat strong ties for novel ideas (40–50% of founders got idea from a contact)
  • Creativity — 5-stage creative process
Idea generation techniques
  • Brainstorming (no criticism, fast, freewheeling)
  • Focus groups (5–10 people, moderator-led)
  • Observation, surveys
  • Library/internet research (BizMiner, ProQuest, IBISWorld, Mintel)
  • Trade shows, customer advisory boards
Idea assessment & opportunity analysis
  • Identify value proposition → talk to customers → assess market → analyse competitors → set financial targets → stress-test
  • Reality check: ~85% of new businesses close within 5 years (usually resources/management/financing, not fraud)
  • 5-phase opportunity analysis: seize → investigate via market research → develop business plan → determine resources → manage distinguishing features
  • Evaluation matrix: market clarity, financial return, competitive advantage, management/risk strength

03 · Entrepreneurship Mindset Skills
4 skill categories
  • Cognitive — spotting gaps, critical thinking, creativity, financial literacy
  • Behavioural — resilience/grit, risk-taking, self-directed learning, resourcefulness
  • Relational — communication/persuasion, collaboration, networking, empathy
  • Mindset foundations — growth mindset, ownership, ethical judgment
Growth vs. fixed mindset (Dweck)
  • Fixed — abilities are static, avoids challenges, gives up easily
  • Growth — abilities develop with effort, embraces challenges, treats failure as feedback
  • Benefits: resilience, perseverance, lasting love of learning
04 · Corporate & Social Entrepreneurship
The "Embattled Corporation" — 4 turbulence sources
  • Customers — fragmented markets, rising expectations
  • Competitors — narrow profitable niches, harder to differentiate
  • Technology — new ways to manage info/production/logistics/sales
  • Legal/regulatory/ethical — more accountability, litigation risk
Corporate entrepreneurship (intrapreneurship)
  • Definition: developing new business ideas/opportunities inside an established corporation
  • 3 components: Innovation + Corporate Venturing + Strategic Renewal
  • Corporate venturing pros: innovation/knowledge transfer, easier external finance, motivated staff. Cons: risky equity, needs venture-mgmt infrastructure, less control
  • Strategic renewal: sustained regeneration, domain redefinition, organisational rejuvenation, business model reconstruction
Building an intrapreneurial culture
  • Set explicit goals, give feedback/positive reinforcement
  • Reward results, don't punish failure
  • Grant autonomy, work discretion, time for future projects
  • Keep teams small (3M rule), stay close to the customer
  • Famous rule-sets: 3M Innovation Rules, IBM EBO Program, "Corporate Innovator's Commandments"
Social entrepreneurship
  • Definition: entrepreneurial drive + primary focus on social impact over profit
  • 4 elements (Robinson): social impact, social innovation, sustainability, measurement
  • NOT social entrepreneurship: pure social service (children's home) or pure activism (Mother Teresa)
  • Bottom of the Pyramid (BOP): >90% of adults in India/Africa in lowest wealth tier vs. ~20% in developed countries — a major underserved target market
05 · Sources of Innovation — Peter Drucker

"The entrepreneur always searches for change, responds to it, and exploits it as an opportunity." — Drucker

Inside the business/industry (sources 1–3)
  • The Unexpected — failure/success/external shock (New Coke 1985; Java Jacket)
  • Incongruity — gap between what is and what's assumed (M-Pesa vs. branch banking)
  • Process Needs — fixing weak workflow spots (ATMs → mobile/agency banking)
Outside the enterprise/industry (sources 4–7)
  • Industry/Market Structure Change — old structure becomes obsolete (Uber vs. taxi dispatch)
  • Demographics — most reliable/predictable trend indicator
  • Changes in Perception, Mood & Meaning — "glass half full vs. half empty"
  • New Knowledge — longest lead time, needs convergence of knowledge streams

Kenyan examples: M-Pesa (2007), Tap2Eat by Wawira Njiru

06 · Disruptive Innovation Theory — Christensen (1997)
Core definition & types
  • Starts niche/underperforming, improves over time, displaces incumbents
  • Low-end disruption — cheaper/simpler, moves upmarket (Netflix DVD → streaming)
  • New-market disruption — creates an entirely new market (Tesla/EVs)
Who it targets first
  • Overserved customers — paying for more than they need
  • Underserved/non-consumers — couldn't access incumbent product at all (M-Pesa/unbanked)
Innovator's Dilemma
  • Sustaining innovation vs. disruptive opportunity trade-off
  • Incumbents deprioritize disruption until it's too late (Kodak & digital photography)
3-stage disruption process
  • Entry (simple/cheap, non-consumers) → Iterative improvement → Upmarket movement (AWS example)
06B · DOI & TAM (theories of adoption) extra topic
Diffusion of Innovation — Rogers (1962)
  • Explains how innovations spread through a population — bell-shaped adoption curve
  • Precondition: perceived novelty, not objective novelty
  • 5-stage decision process: Knowledge → Persuasion → Decision → Implementation → Confirmation
  • 5 adopter categories: Innovators (2.5%) → Early Adopters (13.5%) → Early Majority (34%) → Late Majority (34%) → Laggards (16%)
  • 5 adoption-speed factors: Relative Advantage, Compatibility, Complexity, Trialability, Observability
  • Applied example: M-Pesa's adoption wave; public-health behaviour-change campaigns
Technology Acceptance Model — Davis (1989)
  • Perceived Usefulness (PU) — will this improve my performance?
  • Perceived Ease of Use (PEOU) — will this be effortless?
  • Causal chain: PEOU → PU → Attitude → Behavioral Intention → Actual Use
  • 4 practical benefits: predicts adoption, saves money, guides design, improves change management

07 · Customer Discovery Process
Definition — 3 lenses
  • Investors: due diligence
  • Design thinkers: empathetic, human-centred process
  • Marketers: foundation for resonant messaging
8-step process
  • 1. Identify segments → 2. Form hypotheses → 3. Interviews & surveys → 4. Observe behaviour
  • 5. Iterate on feedback → 6. Build MVP → 7. Test MVP → 8. Analyse & refine

Not a one-off — continues after launch as needs evolve. Worked example: food-delivery startup pivots from "speed" to "quality/temperature" after interviews.

08 · The Lean Startup Methodology heaviest exam weighting
Core loop & concepts
  • Build → Measure → Learn loop (cycles repeatedly)
  • Validated learning — real customer behaviour, not opinion
  • Worked example: Zappos (photographed shoes, bought only after real order)
MVP benefits
  • Quickly validate idea, minimise risk, improve dev focus, gain customer insight, speed to market, attract investors
Key metrics to validate a hypothesis
  • User Acquisition, User Retention, Engagement, Conversion Rate
  • Customer Lifetime Value (CLV), Net Promoter Score (NPS), Cost per Acquisition (CAC/CPA)
Engines of growth
  • Sticky — retention-driven
  • Viral — spreads via customers themselves
  • Paid — sustainable only if CLV > CAC
Related concepts
  • Beachhead market — big enough to matter, small enough to dominate, urgent shared need
  • Market sizing — top-down vs. bottom-up (bottom-up: slow, may underestimate, hard to project growth)
  • Bill Gross factors — Timing, Team/Execution, Idea, Business Model, Funding (timing ranked most important)
Companies built the lean-startup way
  • Dropbox (demo video), Airbnb (own apartment test), Zappos, Spotify, Instagram (pivot), Netflix (DVD→streaming pivot), Slack, GE FastWorks (lean inside a large corporate)
09 · Business Model & Value Proposition Canvas
Business Model Canvas — 9 blocks
  • Customer Segments, Value Proposition, Channels, Customer Relationships, Revenue Streams
  • Key Resources, Key Activities, Key Partners, Cost Structure
Value Proposition Canvas
  • Customer Profile: Jobs, Pains, Gains
  • Value Map: Products/Services, Pain Relievers, Gain Creators
  • "Fit" = value map clearly matches the customer profile's top pains/gains/jobs
  • Value prop statement components: pain + gain + solution + why-better-than-competitors
Business model innovation
  • Rethinking overall strategy (who/how/pricing), not just product features
  • Examples: Netflix, Airbnb, Tesla direct-to-consumer
Types of business models
  • Product-based, Service-based, Subscription-based, Platform-based (multi-sided, e.g. M-Pesa/Uber)
  • Franchise, Freemium (pros: easy acquisition/data; cons: low conversion), Licensing
10 · Lean Canvas Tool — Ash Maurya
9 blocks
  • Problem, Solution, Key Metrics, Unique Value Proposition (centre), Unfair Advantage
  • Channels, Customer Segments, Cost Structure, Revenue Streams

Faster/more testable than a full business plan; one-page, visual, structured for a team to align around. Meant to be revisited as assumptions get tested, not filled once.

11 · Business Idea Feasibility Analysis
4 forms
  • Product/Service — Desirability (concept test) + Demand (talk to customers, AdWords/landing pages)
  • Industry/Target Market — industry attractiveness + target market size/reachability
  • Organizational — management prowess + resource sufficiency (6–12 critical resources)
  • Financial — start-up cash needed, comparable performance, overall attractiveness
12 · How It All Connects
  • 1. Recognize an opportunity (trends / problem / gap)
  • 2. Generate ideas (brainstorming, focus groups, research)
  • 3. Test feasibility (product, market, organizational, financial)
  • 4. Map the business model (Lean Canvas or BMC)
  • 5. Validate with customers (Customer Discovery)
  • 6. Build → Measure → Learn (Lean Startup) toward product-market fit
