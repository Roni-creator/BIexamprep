E-Marketing Fundamentals

Definitions, history of digital marketing, media types, the purchase funnel, and adoption theory.

01 — Core Definitions

Marketing vs e-Marketing

Marketing

  • Activity of promoting and selling products/services, incl. research & advertising
  • Processes for creating, communicating, delivering, exchanging offerings of value
  • Management process for identifying, anticipating, satisfying customer needs — profitably

e-Marketing

  • Using the internet + related tech alongside traditional media to acquire & deliver services
  • Uses websites, mobile, social networking to reach customers, build awareness, sell
  • Connects marketers with consumers to build relationships and drive sales

Exam line to remember: the basics of marketing don't change — it's still about delivering the right message to the right people. Only the channel is new.

History of Digital Marketing

Directly answers: when it started, why it started, key milestones, future direction.

1990s

Term "Digital Marketing" first used. Web 1.0 lets users find info but not share it.

1993

First clickable banner ad goes live; HotWired buys early banner ads.

1994

Yahoo launches ("Jerry's Guide to the World Wide Web") — ~1M hits in year one, pushing SEO-style optimization.

1996

More search engines launch: HotBot, LookSmart, Alexa.

1998

Google is born. Microsoft launches MSN search; Yahoo launches web search.

2000

Dot-com bubble bursts — smaller search engines wiped out, giants dominate.

2004

US internet advertising hits ~$2.9B. Web 2.0 arrives — users become active participants, not passive readers.

2006

Search engine traffic surges to ~6.4B/month. Microsoft launches Live Search.

2003–07

MySpace, then Facebook, arrive — social networking + the cookie (tracking user habits) reshape targeting.

2010

WhatsApp and Google Buzz launch.

2011–12

Google+ and Google Panda launch; 2012 becomes "the year of social media" (Facebook/Myspace dominant).

2013

Yahoo acquires Tumblr.

2014

Mobile/smartphone users outnumber PC users for the first time. Facebook Messenger, LinkedIn & iWatch ads launch; Facebook buys WhatsApp.

2015

Snapchat launches Discover. Analytics, wearables, content marketing all mature.

2017

Facebook, YouTube, Instagram, Twitter, Reddit dominate social.

2022–23

Google retires Expanded Text Ads for automated Responsive Search Ads. Video, creator economy, and generative AI enter marketers' content strategies.

Now

TikTok shifts advertising from brand-first to creator-led. "Social commerce" grows — Facebook/TikTok test live shopping, Pinterest builds shopping tools, X (Twitter) pushes toward a commerce platform.

Segmentation, Tactics, Channels & Content

Market segmentation, buyer personas, targeting, the 31 e-marketing tactics, channels, content marketing, social media marketing, SEO, and CRM.

1. Market Segmentation

Purpose — segmentation checks:
  • What groups of buyers are similar enough that the same product/service appeals to all of them?
  • Does the idea satisfy consumers' needs and add value to existing products?
  • Who's going to buy the product?
  • Will there be enough of these people to make it worth the company's while?

Segmentation categories

CategoryDefinitionExamples
BehavioralHow buyers behave/act toward productsBenefits sought, usage rate, usage situation, buyer status & loyalty
DemographicPersonal characteristicsAge, income, ethnicity, education, occupation, religion, social class, family size
GeographicDivides market by locationRegion, city size, population density, climate
PsychographicDifferentiates by lifestyle/attitudeActivities, interests, opinions, values, lifestyles
FirmographicB2B equivalent of demographics — categorizes companies not individualsCompany size, industry type, annual revenue, number of employees
Exam tip: Remember the mnemonic B-D-G-P-F (Behavioral, Demographic, Geographic, Psychographic, Firmographic). Firmographic = "demographics for businesses."

Multi-segment vs. concentrated marketing

Multi-segment marketing: lets firms respond to demographic changes/trends across several segments.

Concentrated marketing: used by smaller firms with limited resources; targets one very select group (e.g. one-to-one marketing).

Market positioning

Repositioning = moving a product to a different place in consumers' minds.
Perceptual map = two-dimensional graph plotting customer survey data to show where a product stands (or should stand) vs. competitors, based on criteria important to buyers (price, quality, service level, etc.)

2. Marketing Personas

Also called buyer/customer personas — semi-fictional representations of ideal customers based on data & research. They capture demographics, behaviors, motivations, and pain points to guide messaging, product development, and sales strategy.

Typical persona template fields

  • Who they are (age, role, location)
  • Goals / Objectives
  • Pain points / Challenges / Frustrations
  • Content & channel preference (where they get info, how they communicate)
  • How to market to them (messaging approach)
Exam tip: If asked to construct a persona, always include: name, demographic snapshot, goals, pain points, preferred channels, and a marketing approach tailored to those pain points.

3. Targeting / Differentiated Marketing & One-to-One Marketing

Target/differentiated marketing: sells to specific segment(s)/group(s).
Mass marketing: same product to everybody.
One-to-one marketing: personalizes sells — close, personal relationships, giving customers exactly what they want.
Benefits of segmented targeting:
  • Avoid head-on competition
  • Develop new offerings/expand profitable lines
  • Remarket older, less-profitable products
  • Identify early adopters
  • Focus resources on most profitable customers
  • Retain "at-risk" customers

Steps in one-to-one marketing

  1. Establish short-term measures to evaluate efforts (e.g. satisfaction ratings, revenue/customer, transaction costs)
  2. Identify your customers — gather data on buying patterns, likes/dislikes; use "opt-in" to legally collect contact info
  3. Differentiate among customers — determine customer lifetime value, ease of service
  4. Interact with customers — prioritize time/money on high-value, high-growth customers
  5. Customize products and marketing messages

4. eMarketing Tactics (Strategy vs. Tactic)

Strategy = the "what" and "why" (e.g. increase brand awareness for a new launch).
Tactic = the "how" — specific, actionable, short-term methods within a channel (e.g. targeted Instagram ad campaign with a 20% coupon).
Marketing channel = the tool/platform/touchpoint used to communicate with a segment along the customer journey.
Exam tip: A common question is "differentiate strategy from tactic" — Strategy = direction/goal; Tactic = the concrete action executing it.

5. E-Marketing Channels

Channels = where your marketing happens — the platforms/mediums used to reach an audience online ("the roads you use to deliver your message"). They involve the people, organizations, and activities that move goods from production to consumption.

Types of e-marketing channels

  • Content marketing — creation & distribution of content for a specific audience
  • SEO — obtaining high-quality traffic from free/organic search results
  • SEM — combines SEO + PPC (paid + free search traffic)
  • SMM (Social Media Marketing) — attracting traffic/attention via social platforms
  • Affiliate promotion — incentivizing influencers to share positive sentiment
  • Email marketing — using email to promote products/services
  • Instant messaging marketing — e.g. WhatsApp (1.5B+ monthly users), Messenger, WeChat
  • Network marketing — leverages associations/groups online; identifying influencers/wholesalers to spread word

7. Social Media Marketing (SMM)

5 steps to successful social media marketing

  1. Define your goal — traffic, sales, brand awareness, leads, engagement, customer service, branded content, community, promotions
  2. Understand your customers (segmentation) — age, gender, education, occupation, needs, hobbies, location
  3. Competitor analysis — identify social competitors & their campaign strength
  4. Pick your social channels — start with 1–2 platforms ("efficiency vs. effectiveness")

8. Search Engine Optimization (SEO)

Goal: help a website rank higher in organic (unpaid) search results. Keyword research finds the trigger words your target audience searches for.

How SEO works (3 steps)

  1. Crawling — Google's crawlers/spiders follow hyperlinks to discover content
  2. Indexing — unique content is added to Google's search index
  3. Ranking — results ordered by relevance using the algorithm's ranking factors

Three types of SEO

TypeFocus
On-site (on-page)Optimizing content for users & search engines (keyword research, quality content, keyword placement, titles, meta descriptions, images, internal/external links)
Off-siteBrand assets (people, marks, values, slogans), reputation via listings, press, reviews
TechnicalBack-end optimizations for site security & user experience (e.g. page speed)

On-page optimization checklist

  1. Keyword research
  2. Create quality content targeting those keywords
  3. Place keywords in specific spots
  4. Optimize titles (1 H1 tag/page, 55–60 characters)
  5. Optimize meta descriptions
  6. Optimize images (compress, size)
  7. Internal links (to your own posts) + external links (1–3 relevant pages)
Exam tip: Users want info in under 3 seconds — slow pages risk losing visitors to competitors. Know this stat if asked about page speed.

Uses / benefits of SEO

  • Brings organic search traffic (long-term, most preferred vs. paid)
  • Generates leads cost-effectively
  • Increases brand awareness
  • Gets you ahead of competition
  • Optimizes for mobile users
  • Builds trust

SEO tools

  • Paid SEO software: Ahrefs, Moz Pro, Screaming Frog, SEMrush
  • Website graders: simplify SEO, offer guidance
  • Google Analytics: free — traffic, time on page, engagement, sessions
  • Google Search Console (GSC): content/technical SEO — Core Web Vitals, query analysis, indexing
  • Keyword research tools: find realistic targets by volume & competition

CAT1 — Case Study: Selling Life Insurance

Case: Mark sells a bundled insurance/savings product (high commission 110%, low 3–6% return) vs. a cheaper, better-value product paired with an annuity (low commission, discouraged by management).

a) Five reasons an organisation needs an e-Marketing strategy 10 marks

  1. Optimal resource allocation — spend goes to the highest-ROI channels.
  2. Targeted audience reach — reach the exact demographic likely to convert.
  3. Competitive advantage — spot market gaps competitors miss.
  4. Data-driven decision making — replaces guesswork with KPIs/analytics.

Remember also: without a strategy you risk being directionless, having poor digital maturity, losing to competitors, not knowing your online audience/market share, not understanding customers deeply enough, and wasting money through duplication.

b) Main problem at Mark's company 2 marks

Beyond the ethical tension (cheap/customer-favouring product vs. expensive/company-favouring product), the core marketing issue is revenue growth — the company is prioritising profit/market share over customer value.

c) Best model for situational analysis 4 marks

SWOT is most suitable (4Ps also acceptable). It structures internal factors (Strengths/Weaknesses) and external factors (Opportunities/Threats) to build a data-driven strategy while managing risk.

d) SWOT Analysis of the situation 8 marks

Strengths

Customisable, marketable products

Weaknesses

High cost of products; age-sensitive product design

Opportunities

Modify less-profitable products; tech advancement enabling digital marketing

Threats

New entrants/competition; changing workforce trends (early retirement, younger staff)

e) Value proposition 1 mark

"Financial protection and peace of mind" — shifting the burden of catastrophic financial loss from the individual to a pool of policyholders in exchange for a predictable premium.

Lecture 5: Digital Marketing Analytics

What is it?

A math-based discipline that finds patterns in marketing data to generate actionable knowledge that improves marketing performance.

Methods: Marketing mix modeling, Demand forecasting, Competitor analytics, Unmet needs analytics, Trend analytics.

High-quality data must be:

  • Current
  • Comprehensive (no gaps)
  • Error-free
  • As precise as required
  • Relevant to the analysis

B2B vs B2C

  • B2C: appeals to emotional reaction → transactional purchase.
  • B2B: builds brand recognition/relationships → leads → sales. Relies on keyword analysis, target market data, lead scoring, lead-to-customer ratio.

Types of Analytics

  • Web analytics — measurement/collection/analysis/reporting of internet data to optimize web usage.
  • Clickstream analytics — tracks: unique/repeat visitor, search terms used, first landing page, time on page, features clicked, cart add/remove, next page, back-button use.
  • Conversion analytics — a conversion = any counted user action (purchase, level completion, scroll, video play, signup, free trial, form fill).

4 Key Areas of Email Marketing Metrics

  • Delivery — getting to the inbox
  • Engagement — how people interact with your message
  • Conversion — business impact
  • List Health — audience growth & fatigue

ROI Formulas — Memorise These

Google Ads Campaign ROI

ROI = (Revenue − Cost of Goods Sold) / Cost of Goods Sold

SEO ROI (concept)

Calculate ROI for each SEO tactic (tools + personnel cost). Track sales funnel conversions by phase, then subtract revenue generated from investment made.

Return on Ad Spend (ROAS)

ROAS = Total Revenue / Total Ad Spend

Tells you revenue earned for every $1 spent on ads.

Cost per Result

Cost per Result = Total Ad Spend / Number of Results

Result Rate (Conversion Rate)

Result Rate = Number of Results / Number of Impressions

CPM (Cost per 1000 Impressions)

Cost of achieving 1,000 impressions — measures cost-effectiveness of an ad campaign.

CTR (Click-Through-Rate)

CTR = Clicks / Impressions

100 clicks sounds good — but out of 100,000 impressions it's a weak CTR. Always read clicks relative to impressions.

1. Marketing Frameworks & Strategy

Overall campaign goal Aug'24 Q1a2 marks
For the iSpionage case, the overall goal is to increase referral traffic from the company blog to the main website, so that more blog readers convert into users of iSpionage's competitive-intelligence tool — i.e. turning content marketing into qualified traffic and leads rather than just readership.
Why marketers need a clear goal Dec'24 Q1a2 marks
  • A clear goal gives the campaign direction and lets every tactic (CRM automation, content, workflows) be judged against whether it moves the business toward that goal, avoiding wasted spend on activity that "feels" productive but doesn't serve the objective.
  • It provides a benchmark for measuring success (e.g. Handled's 90-day launch goal), so performance can be tracked and the campaign adjusted if it drifts off target.
7-S Marketing Mix framework Aug'24 Q1b · Dec'24 Q1b11 / 8 marks
The 7S framework analyses a digital campaign's situation under seven elements. Apply each to the case, stating assumptions where the case is silent:
  • Strategy – the overall direction (e.g. Handled's strategy of a tech-enabled, personalised concierge moving service on one unified CRM platform).
  • Site – the company's website/online presence used to reach customers.
  • Scope – the breadth of online activity: which products/services and markets are covered digitally.
  • Synergy – how online activity integrates with offline operations (e.g. concierge reps supported by CRM automation).
  • System – the technology stack/processes running the campaign (e.g. HubSpot's Marketing, Sales, Service and CMS Hubs).
  • Structure – how the marketing/digital team and reporting lines are organised.
  • Staff – skills and resourcing needed (e.g. HubSpot consultants, concierge service reps).
State assumptions explicitly, e.g. "assuming the company has an in-house digital team" — assumptions earn credit because the case text doesn't cover every S.
SMART marketing objectives Aug'24 Q1c · Dec'24 Q1c · SafariSip Q1ii4 / 4 / 2 marks
Objectives must be Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound. Examples:
  • iSpionage: "Increase blog-to-main-site referral traffic by 58% within 1 month using an exit-intent popup."
  • Handled: "Launch the full HubSpot CRM (Marketing, Sales, Service, CMS Hubs) within 90 days to unify sales, marketing and service."
  • SafariSip (from its stated strengths/weaknesses): "Increase email subscribers from the current base to 10,000 active coffee enthusiasts within 12 months" and "Improve website conversion rate by X% within 6 months by upgrading site speed and adding product videos."
SafariSip: one strength, two weaknesses (SOSTAC – Situation) SafariSip Q1i3 marks
  • Strength: A high-quality, well-reviewed product with an established multi-channel presence (website + Instagram/Facebook/TikTok with 12,000 combined followers).
  • Weakness 1: A slow, basic website with limited analytics and no product videos — 68% of surveyed customers called it "uninspiring" or "slow", which hurts conversion.
  • Weakness 2: Uncoordinated marketing — social posting has no consistent schedule/tracking, email is used without segmentation, and 40% of respondents didn't even know an online store existed.

3. SEO & Organic Search

Organic vs paid search SafariSip Q3i2 marks
Organic search results appear because the search engine's algorithm ranks the page as relevant — no fee is paid to appear, but it takes time and SEO effort. Paid search results (marked "Ad") appear because the advertiser bids on the keyword and pays per click; placement is immediate but stops the moment the budget runs out. For "coffee in Nairobi", SafariSip could run a paid campaign now while SEO work improves organic ranking over time.
Three techniques to raise organic SERP ranking SafariSip Q3ii6 marks
  • On-page keyword optimisation: use "coffee in Nairobi" naturally in titles, headers, meta descriptions, and page copy.
  • Technical SEO / site speed: fix the slow-loading WordPress site, add proper analytics/schema markup, and ensure mobile-friendliness — all direct ranking factors.
  • Local SEO & backlinks: claim a Google Business Profile for a Nairobi location and earn backlinks from Kenyan food/lifestyle sites to build authority and local relevance.
Five SEO ranking factors Aug'24 Q1f5 marks
  • Quality and relevance of content (keyword match to search intent)
  • Backlink quantity and quality (domain authority pointing to the site)
  • Site speed and mobile-friendliness
  • Domain authority / trustworthiness (HTTPS, age of domain)
  • User engagement signals (click-through rate, dwell time, bounce rate)
On-page vs off-page optimisation Aug'24 Q3d4 marks
On-page SEO is everything controlled directly on the website — content quality, keyword placement, meta tags, headers, internal linking, page speed. Off-page SEO happens outside the site and builds external authority — backlinks, social shares, influencer mentions, online PR/citations.
Three types of SEO Dec'24 Q4b3 marks
  • On-page SEO – optimising content and HTML elements on the page itself.
  • Off-page SEO – earning authority through backlinks, PR and social signals.
  • Technical SEO – improving crawlability, indexing, site speed and mobile usability.
SEO vs SEM (with examples) Aug'24 Q5c4 marks
SEO (Search Engine Optimisation) is the practice of earning free/organic rankings through content and technical improvements — e.g. optimising a blog post so it ranks for "Kenyan coffee beans." SEM (Search Engine Marketing) is the broader paid discipline of buying visibility through PPC ads — e.g. running a Google Ads campaign bidding on "buy Kenyan coffee online." SEO is slower but free per click; SEM is fast but costs per click.
Backlinks and how they help SEO/PPC performance Aug'24 Q2c4 marks
A backlink is a link from another website to yours. Search engines treat backlinks (especially from reputable, relevant sites) as a vote of confidence, raising domain authority and organic rank. Example: if a well-known coffee blog links to SafariSip's brewing-tutorial page, that page is more likely to rank higher for "how to brew Kenyan coffee," bringing in free qualified traffic that would otherwise need to be paid for via PPC.
Google AdWords' contribution to campaigns Aug'24 Q2d3 marks
Google Ads (AdWords) lets marketers bid on keywords so ads appear instantly at the top of search results, giving immediate visibility that organic SEO takes months to earn — e.g. SafariSip could bid on "buy Kenyan coffee online" to appear above page-2 organic listings straight away, and use conversion tracking to measure ROI directly.
Robots exclusion protocol SafariSip Q2iv2 marks
The robots exclusion protocol (robots.txt) is a file placed at a website's root that tells search-engine crawlers which pages or directories they may or may not index. It matters because it lets a site control crawl budget and keep private/duplicate pages (e.g. admin or checkout pages) out of search results, while ensuring important pages remain crawlable and rankable.

4. PPC, Paid Search & Display Advertising

Long-tail vs short-tail keywords SafariSip Q5iv4 marks
Short-tail keywords (e.g. "coffee") are broad, one or two words, with high search volume but high competition and low buyer intent — good for broad brand awareness. Long-tail keywords (e.g. "fine organic Kenyan coffee") are longer, more specific phrases with lower volume but higher intent and conversion rate, and lower cost-per-click — better for PPC campaigns aiming at ready-to-buy customers.
Keyword matching techniques: negative match & phrase match SafariSip Q5v4 marks
Negative match excludes the ad from showing for specified irrelevant terms — e.g. adding "-free" or "-recipe" as negative keywords stops the ad appearing for people searching for free coffee or coffee recipes, saving budget from unqualified clicks. Phrase match shows the ad for searches containing the exact phrase in that order, with extra words allowed before/after — e.g. "fine organic Kenyan coffee" would match "buy fine organic Kenyan coffee online," balancing reach with relevance (tighter than broad match, looser than exact match).
Reasons Google may reject an ad Aug'24 Q3b2 marks
  • The ad violates content policy (e.g. misleading claims, prohibited products, inappropriate content).
  • The landing page is broken, has poor user experience, or doesn't match the ad's claims (destination mismatch).
Five hallmarks of a good PPC landing page Aug'24 Q3c5 marks
  • Message match — headline mirrors the ad's promise/keyword.
  • Single, clear call-to-action (CTA).
  • Fast load speed and mobile responsiveness.
  • Minimal distractions/navigation to keep focus on converting.
  • Trust signals (reviews, security badges, testimonials).
Types of banners besides display banners SafariSip Q4iii3 marks
  • Pop-up/interstitial banners
  • Expandable (rich media) banners
  • Sticky/floating banners (e.g. anchored footer bar)
Banner ad design brief — 50% off roast coffee beans, November sale SafariSip Q4iv5 marks
A hand-drawn/described concept, since this is a design task: a rectangular display banner (e.g. 300×250px) with a warm brown/cream background; the SafariSip steaming-cup-of-coffee logo top-left; large bold headline "50% OFF ROAST BEANS" centred; a supporting line "November Only — SafariSip Coffee"; and a single orange CTA button reading "Shop Now" bottom-right, linking to the online store's sale page. Keep text minimal and the CTA visually dominant, per standard banner best practice (clear offer, brand mark, one action).

12. Market Research & Segmentation

Primary vs secondary research to understand competitors SafariSip Q2i6 marks
Primary research — data SafariSip collects itself, e.g. mystery-shopping competitor websites, surveying customers on why they chose a rival brand, or interviewing lapsed customers. This gives fresh, specific insight but takes time and cost. Secondary research — using existing published data, e.g. industry reports, competitors' public social media metrics, or SEO tools like SEMrush to see competitors' keyword rankings and ad strategies. This is cheaper and faster but less tailored to SafariSip's exact questions. Using both together (as the case's competitor analysis suggests) gives a fuller picture of why rivals use SEO, video and email automation effectively.
Three types of questions the research team may have used in surveys SafariSip Q5vi3 marks
  • Closed/multiple-choice questions — e.g. "How did you discover SafariSip?" with fixed options (social media, search, referral).
  • Rating/Likert-scale questions — e.g. rating website experience from "very poor" to "excellent."
  • Open-ended questions — e.g. "What would improve your experience with SafariSip's website?" allowing free-text feedback.
Four buyer characteristics for market segmentation Dec'24 Q2a8 marks

Active vs passive customers Dec'24 Q1e2 marks
Active customers engage regularly with the brand — recent purchasers, frequent visitors/repeat buyers. Passive customers are aware of or have purchased from the brand before but are not currently engaging — dormant subscribers or one-time buyers who need re-engagement campaigns to reactivate.
Keyword search in digital marketing Dec'24 Q1f2 marks
Keyword search is the process customers use to type relevant terms into search engines to find products/services; marketers research and target these exact terms (via SEO and PPC) so their content/ads appear when potential customers are actively searching.
Why demand for targeted marketing is rising Dec'24 Q1g6 marks
  • Growing volumes of customer data (from web/CRM analytics) make precise targeting practically possible.
  • Customers increasingly expect personalised, relevant experiences and disengage from generic mass messaging.
  • Targeted campaigns deliver better ROI, since budget is spent on audiences most likely to convert rather than broad, wasteful reach.
Two benefits of mass marketing to firms Dec'24 Q2d2 marks

15. E-Marketing Trends, AI/ML & Adoption Challenges

Internet, AI & ML influence on the growth of e-marketing Dec'24 Q3a5 marks

Simple ROI assumes all sales growth in a period is caused by the marketing campaign, but sales are also influenced by seasonality, word-of-mouth, competitor actions and other factors — so attributing 100% of growth to marketing overstates the campaign's true impact.

16. Campaign Management Lifecycle

Seven critical phases of creating & managing a digital marketing campaign Dec'24 Q5c7 marks
  1. Situation analysis — assess current position (e.g. SOSTAC's "Situation").
  2. Objective setting — define SMART goals.
  3. Strategy formulation — decide the overall approach to reach objectives.
  4. Tactics selection — choose specific channels/activities (SEO, email, social, PPC).
  5. Action/implementation planning — assign resources, timelines and responsibilities.
  6. Execution/launch — run the campaign across chosen channels.
  7. Control/evaluation — monitor KPIs, measure results, and optimise continuously.
